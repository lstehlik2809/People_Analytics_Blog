How a choice experiment with 423 quality control operators can inform employee listening and AI-driven job redesign.

Luděk Stehlík https://www.linkedin.com/in/ludekstehlik/
2026-11-18

What if an employee survey asked you to pick between two versions of your own job?

Lara Fleck and colleagues did something along those lines with 423 quality control operators at an automotive supplier. The operators saw pairs of hypothetical jobs and picked the one they thought would be better for their well-being. The options varied in what they’d do on a shift, when they could take breaks, what training they’d get, how much say they’d have over their tasks, and what they’d earn.

The choices were based on an actual AI rollout. In the first stage, a pop-up asked operators to take another look when the system disagreed with them. The operator still made the call. In the planned second stage, the system would clear the easy cases, leaving operators with fewer, harder ones.

Workers preferred the version where they got a second opinion. The 6% raise in the example above was part of one hypothetical job offer. The chart below summarizes choices across the experiment. From those choices, the researchers estimated that workers valued AI-assisted inspection about as much as a 2.8% raise in the old job without AI.

What I like about this as a form of employee listening is how specific you can get. Say you’re about to automate the routine checks. Would people be more interested in the redesigned job if they got more practice on the difficult cases? How much would it matter if they could decide when to switch to another task?

I’d work out a few realistic options with the people doing the job, then use their choices to help decide what to try on the floor.

P.S. I focused on the survey design here, but it struck me that the operators who found the AI assistant especially helpful were less keen on the more automated job. A good reminder that when you roll out a change in stages, it’s worth asking people how they’d feel about the next step, even if they’re happy with how things are going so far.
