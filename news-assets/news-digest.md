### [Digital inclusion and labor market performance: an experimental evaluation](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13209-026-00349-w>)

SERIEs · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-24

A randomized trial assigned more than 2,900 disadvantaged adults aged 45–64 to digital access, access plus training, or a control group; skills and job-search gains did not translate into detectable employment gains, including in administrative records. Six-month follow-up strengthens the evaluation, although employment effects below approximately 3.5 percentage points could remain undetected.

**Why it matters for practice** — When evaluating digital upskilling or employment-support programs for older job seekers, measure actual employment separately from digital proficiency and search activity. Do not treat improvements in intermediate outcomes as sufficient evidence of placement impact.

### [Interpersonal incivility among trauma team members impairs performance and patient outcomes in crisis care settings](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73686-2_reference.pdf>)

Scientific Reports · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-24

Real-time observations of trauma resuscitations linked team incivility to slower diagnostic imaging and greater patient pain, with team-level analyses covering 52 teams and individual-level observations covering 59 resuscitations. The connection to operational and patient outcomes makes this useful field evidence, but the observational design cannot establish causation.

**Why it matters for practice** — In crisis-care workforce analytics, supplement employee climate surveys with event-level behavioral observations linked to treatment latency and patient outcomes. Evaluate civility initiatives against these operational measures rather than assuming that better survey scores demonstrate better performance.

### [Using Process Data to Understand Faking in Questionnaires](<https://kar.kent.ac.uk/116535/13/frick-et-al-2026-using-process-data-to-understand-faking-in-questionnaires.pdf>)

Diagnostica · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-26

A preregistered reanalysis of five questionnaire datasets modeled response times and edits using cross-classified models and independently rated item desirability. Results illustrate an approach to assessment-process analysis, but mostly unsuccessful stakes manipulations and inconsistent effects substantially limit conclusions about detecting faking.

**Why it matters for practice** — When auditing personality assessments used in selection, model item and block characteristics before interpreting fast responses or answer changes as suspicious. Treat such process measures as exploratory diagnostic evidence, not as validated grounds for rejecting applicants.

### [Correcting Bias When Using Latent Regressors](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/correcting-bias-when-using-latent-regressors/C6C42CD8A39396D3A2915471F68D5CD6>)

Political Analysis · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-24

The authors derive a split-indicator correction for regression models using estimated latent traits, comparing it with conventional regression and alternative corrections through simulations and empirical applications. It reduced bias under the studied assumptions, although confidence intervals could undercover with few indicators and the method requires suitable independent measurement errors across indicator splits.

**Why it matters for practice** — When using multi-item engagement or psychological-safety scores as predictors in a linear model, compare conventional estimates with a measurement-error-corrected sensitivity analysis before ranking their associations with outcomes. Check whether the items support comparable indicator splits; do not automatically apply the correction to multidimensional scales or interpret corrected associations as causal effects.

### [Can AI Judge You? An Empirical Audit of Deployment Readiness in LLM-assisted Hiring Systems](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20140>)

arXiv · Preprint · 2026-11-15

This preprint tested four LLMs using 210 constructed resumes across 14 job roles and six decision interfaces, alongside tests of unauthorized instructions. Scalar scores generally detected qualification changes better than pass/fail judgments, while comparative judgments were order-sensitive; the controlled audit supports configuration testing, not conclusions about actual hiring validity.

**Why it matters for practice** — Before deploying an LLM resume screener, test whether controlled additions or removals of job-relevant qualifications change its assessments appropriately, and repeat comparisons with candidate order reversed. Separately test whether resume-embedded instructions or unauthorized requests can change screening rules, rather than treating demographic parity or plausible explanations as sufficient evidence of readiness.

### [The Dark Side of Mindfulness at Work: How Mindfulness Training Enlivens Machiavellian Incivility](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-026-06513-3>)

Journal of Business Ethics · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-12

A randomized workplace field experiment with 473 analyzed participants compared mindfulness training with matched breathing exercises and a waitlist, finding a conditional indirect association with self-reported incivility among employees higher in Machiavellianism. Active controls and alternative-mechanism checks strengthen the evidence, but one employer, one program, and self-reported outcomes limit generalization and leave reporting changes possible.

**Why it matters for practice** — When evaluating workplace mindfulness programs, measure interpersonal mistreatment alongside intended benefits and examine variation rather than reporting average improvements alone. Add confidential coworker feedback or behavioral indicators where feasible; these findings warrant monitoring unintended consequences, not personality screening or restricting employee access.

### [A differences-in-differences study of staff experience in the English NHS after investment in digital technologies](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-026-01999-4_reference.pdf>)

Communications Medicine · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-21

Comparing 26 funded with 77 unfunded NHS trusts across 2018–2023, researchers found less favourable staff-experience scores associated with real-time location system funding, but no detectable associations for automation and AI funding. Event-study checks and entropy balancing strengthen the comparison, although nonrandom funding allocation, organisation-level data and short follow-up limit causal interpretation.

**Why it matters for practice** — When evaluating workplace technology rollouts, separate technologies rather than pooling them into one digital-investment indicator, and track stressors, engagement and motivation alongside productivity. Establish comparison groups and inspect pre-intervention trends before attributing changes to the rollout.

### [Multiple Sources, Mixed Signals: How Supervisor and Coworker Undermining (In)congruence Affects Employee Deviance](<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/job.70137?af=R>)

Journal of Organizational Behavior · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-20

Two field studies linked discrepancies between supervisor and coworker undermining with lower thriving and counterproductive behaviour: an eight-week US study provided 343 observations from 86 employees, and a preregistered Chinese study covered 403 employees in 122 teams. Replication across within-person and between-person designs supports examining these sources jointly, but self-reported observational data do not establish the proposed causal pathway.

**Why it matters for practice** — In employee-listening analyses, measure supervisor and coworker undermining separately and examine their joint pattern rather than averaging them into an overall mistreatment score. When investigating poor team experiences, do not assume favourable treatment from a manager offsets undermining by peers.

### [Leading an age-diverse workforce: evaluation of a leadership training intervention based on the Include, Individualize, and Integrate framework](<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10869-026-10166-4>)

Journal of Business and Psychology · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-21

A pre/post evaluation comparing 35 trained leaders with 19 controls found greater knowledge gains and reduced negative age stereotypes, but no significant group-by-time improvements in the three leadership behaviours assessed. Separate team-member ratings detected no improvements in age bias, psychological safety, intergenerational contact or information exchange; the small, nonrandomized study offers a useful evaluation template rather than definitive evidence of effectiveness.

**Why it matters for practice** — When piloting age-inclusive leadership training, distinguish satisfaction and knowledge acquisition from behavioural transfer and employee experience. Include comparison leaders and delayed team-member assessments before treating positive participant feedback or within-group gains as evidence that the programme changed workplace outcomes.

Organization Science · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-13

A two-year ethnographic comparison of two teams found that apparent openness could coexist with continued avoidance of interpersonal risk. ### [HireWatch: Evaluating LLM Compliance with U.S. Employment Law Under Contextual Pressure](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24970>)

arXiv · Preprint · 2026-11-16

This preprint tested 18 models on interview-question selection using a 200-item bank audited by nine licensed U.S. attorneys; simulated organizational pressure increased selection of questions classified as unlawful. Its factorial comparisons and compliance-prompt tests make the benchmark useful, but fixed-choice tasks and context-dependent legal labels limit generalization to live interviews.

**Why it matters for practice** — Before deploying AI interview assistance, test it with realistic manager requests and internal policy documents, not just neutral prompts. Compare explicit compliance instructions against the baseline and retain qualified human review rather than treating a low benchmark violation rate as legal clearance.

### [Personality Was Robust to the Transition to Remote Work Under the COVID‐19 Pandemic](<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jopy.70104>)

Journal of Personality · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-16

Longitudinal archival data from 1,475 working adults showed no evidence that the transition to remote work changed personality-item interpretation or average trait scores. Combining item-response-theory invariance analyses with mixed-effects models helps distinguish measurement changes from trait changes, although this was not a randomized work-location experiment.

**Why it matters for practice** — When interpreting personality assessments across a move to remote work, do not assume that the location change alone requires new norms or explains score shifts. Check invariance and longitudinal patterns in the relevant workforce; this study does not establish that an assessment's job-performance validity transfers unchanged.

### [Different but the same? Internal structure, measurement invariance, DIF, and reliability evidence across test modes](<https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1961544/full>)

Frontiers in Psychology · Peer-reviewed research · 2026-11-16

Comparisons of student assessments across online and face-to-face administration found that acceptable factor fit or similar reliability did not guarantee comparable scores. Ordinal invariance tests, item-level effect sizes and differential-item-functioning analyses provide a useful worked example, but nonrandom groups prevent attributing the differences to administration mode.

**Why it matters for practice** — Before pooling assessment results across delivery modes or interpreting a change after digitizing a survey, test the level of invariance needed for the intended comparison rather than checking reliability alone. The supplied analysis syntax can inform a local validation study, but these student findings do not validate any employee instrument.
